## Overview

The review and approval process is the systematic evaluation of proponent submissions by EAO staff to ensure compliance with environmental certificate conditions and regulatory requirements. This process balances thoroughness with efficiency, providing clear pathways for approval while identifying issues that need correction.

### Review Objectives

1. **Verify Compliance**: Ensure submissions meet certificate conditions
2. **Assess Adequacy**: Evaluate whether proposed measures are sufficient
3. **Identify Gaps**: Find missing information or inadequate approaches
4. **Provide Feedback**: Give clear guidance on needed improvements
5. **Make Decisions**: Approve, reject, or request revisions
6. **Maintain Standards**: Ensure consistent evaluation across submissions

### Review Principles

**Transparency**: Proponents understand what's being evaluated and why
**Consistency**: Similar submissions receive similar treatment
**Timeliness**: Reviews completed within reasonable timeframes
**Collaboration**: Open communication between EAO and proponents
**Documentation**: All decisions and rationale clearly recorded

## Review Stages

The review process consists of three main stages, each with specific objectives and outcomes.

### Stage Overview

```
Package Submitted
       ↓
[Stage 1: Consultation Check]
       ↓
[Stage 2: Technical Review]
       ↓
[Stage 3: Manager Approval]
       ↓
Final Decision
```

### Stage 1: Consultation Check

**Purpose**: Verify that Indigenous consultation requirements have been met.

**Duration**: Typically 5-10 business days

**Reviewer**: Consultation specialist or designated EAO staff

**Focus Areas**:
- Consultation activities documented
- Indigenous concerns identified and addressed
- Consultation commitments fulfilled
- Ongoing relationship maintained

**Outcomes**:
- **Pass**: Consultation adequate, proceed to technical review
- **Fail**: Consultation inadequate, revision required

### Stage 2: Technical Review

**Purpose**: Detailed evaluation of technical content and compliance.

**Duration**: Typically 30-60 business days depending on complexity

**Reviewers**: Subject matter experts in relevant disciplines

**Focus Areas**:
- Technical adequacy of proposed measures
- Compliance with certificate conditions
- Appropriateness of monitoring programs
- Feasibility of commitments
- Quality and completeness of information

**Outcomes**:
- **Satisfactory**: Proceed to manager approval
- **Issues Identified**: Request revisions or additional information
- **Serious Concerns**: Recommend rejection

### Stage 3: Manager Approval

**Purpose**: Final decision by senior EAO staff.

**Duration**: Typically 5-15 business days

**Reviewer**: EAO manager or director

**Focus Areas**:
- Review of technical assessment
- Consideration of broader policy implications
- Assessment of risk
- Final approval decision

**Outcomes**:
- **Approved**: Submission accepted
- **Rejected**: Submission not accepted
- **Additional Review Needed**: Return to technical review

## Consultation Check Process

### What is Being Evaluated

**Consultation Activities**:
- Meetings held with Indigenous groups
- Correspondence exchanged
- Site visits conducted
- Information sharing sessions
- Capacity funding provided

**Issue Identification**:
- Indigenous concerns clearly documented
- Issues organized by theme
- Severity and urgency noted
- Source of concerns identified

**Proponent Responses**:
- How each concern was addressed
- Commitments made
- Accommodations provided
- Rationale for decisions

**Ongoing Engagement**:
- Plans for future consultation
- Relationship maintenance activities
- Dispute resolution mechanisms
- Long-term engagement strategy

### Consultation Check Steps

**Step 1: Initial Assessment**

**Reviewer Actions**:
1. Review consultation record package
2. Check for required documentation
3. Verify consultation activities occurred
4. Assess completeness of record

**Evaluation Criteria**:
- All required consultation activities documented
- Timeline of activities is clear
- Participants identified
- Methods appropriate for context

**Step 2: Issue Analysis**

**Reviewer Actions**:
1. Identify Indigenous concerns raised
2. Assess significance of issues
3. Evaluate proponent responses
4. Determine if concerns adequately addressed

**Evaluation Criteria**:
- All concerns documented
- Responses are substantive, not dismissive
- Commitments are specific and measurable
- Follow-through is evident

**Step 3: Adequacy Assessment**

**Reviewer Actions**:
1. Assess overall quality of consultation
2. Evaluate meaningfulness of engagement
3. Consider Indigenous perspectives
4. Determine if consultation obligations met

**Evaluation Criteria**:
- Consultation was meaningful, not just procedural
- Indigenous input influenced decisions
- Relationship is respectful and ongoing
- Legal obligations fulfilled

**Step 4: Decision**

**Pass Criteria**:
- Required consultation activities completed
- Indigenous concerns documented and addressed
- Commitments are being fulfilled
- Ongoing engagement planned

**Fail Criteria**:
- Missing required consultation activities
- Indigenous concerns not adequately addressed
- Commitments not being met
- Consultation appears superficial

### Common Consultation Check Issues

**Issue 1: Inadequate Documentation**
- **Problem**: Consultation activities occurred but not well documented
- **Solution**: Provide detailed meeting notes, attendance lists, correspondence

**Issue 2: Unaddressed Concerns**
- **Problem**: Indigenous concerns raised but no proponent response
- **Solution**: Provide specific responses to each concern, explain decisions

**Issue 3: Lack of Follow-Through**
- **Problem**: Commitments made but not fulfilled
- **Solution**: Demonstrate implementation of commitments, provide evidence

**Issue 4: Superficial Engagement**
- **Problem**: Consultation appears to be "checking boxes" rather than meaningful
- **Solution**: Demonstrate how Indigenous input influenced decisions, show ongoing relationship

## Technical Review Process

### Review Assignment

**How Reviewers are Assigned**:
1. Package submitted to EAO
2. Review coordinator assesses package type and content
3. Appropriate subject matter experts identified
4. Reviewers assigned based on expertise and workload
5. Reviewers notified of assignment

**Reviewer Qualifications**:
- Subject matter expertise in relevant discipline
- Knowledge of certificate conditions
- Understanding of regulatory requirements
- Experience with similar projects

**Multiple Reviewers**:
- Complex packages may have multiple reviewers
- Each reviewer focuses on their area of expertise
- Reviews coordinated by lead reviewer
- Consolidated feedback provided to proponent

### Item-by-Item Review

**Review Approach**:
Each item in the package is reviewed independently, allowing for:
- Focused evaluation of specific topics
- Clear identification of issues
- Targeted feedback
- Independent status tracking

**For Each Item, Reviewer Evaluates**:

**Completeness**:
- All required information provided
- No significant gaps
- Supporting documentation included
- References and citations appropriate

**Accuracy**:
- Information is correct and current
- Data is reliable
- Calculations are accurate
- Facts are verifiable

**Adequacy**:
- Proposed measures are sufficient
- Monitoring is appropriate
- Mitigation is effective
- Commitments are achievable

**Compliance**:
- Meets certificate conditions
- Aligns with regulatory requirements
- Consistent with previous approvals
- Addresses any outstanding issues

### Review Documentation

**Review Notes**:
Reviewers create notes for each item, including:
- Summary of content reviewed
- Assessment of adequacy
- Issues or concerns identified
- Questions for proponent
- Recommendations for approval/revision

**Note Types**:

**Internal Notes** (Staff Only):
- Preliminary observations
- Discussion points for team
- Sensitive information
- Draft recommendations

**External Notes** (Visible to Proponent):
- Specific issues identified
- Questions requiring response
- Guidance on improvements
- Clear action items

### Technical Review Steps

**Step 1: Initial Review**

**Reviewer Actions**:
1. Review package overview and metadata
2. Understand project context
3. Review relevant certificate conditions
4. Identify key areas of focus

**Duration**: 1-2 days

**Step 2: Detailed Evaluation**

**Reviewer Actions**:
1. Review each item thoroughly
2. Check documents and forms
3. Verify data and calculations
4. Assess compliance with conditions
5. Identify issues and gaps
6. Document findings in review notes

**Duration**: 2-4 weeks depending on complexity

**Step 3: Consultation with Colleagues**

**Reviewer Actions**:
1. Discuss findings with other reviewers
2. Seek input from specialists
3. Coordinate feedback
4. Resolve any conflicting assessments

**Duration**: 1 week

**Step 4: Prepare Recommendations**

**Reviewer Actions**:
1. Consolidate all findings
2. Categorize issues by severity
3. Determine if revision needed
4. Draft recommendation for manager
5. Prepare feedback for proponent

**Duration**: 3-5 days

**Step 5: Communicate Decision**

**Reviewer Actions**:
1. Update package status
2. Send feedback to proponent
3. Provide clear guidance on next steps
4. Set expectations for timeline

**Duration**: 1-2 days

### Review Criteria by Package Type

#### Management Plans

**Key Evaluation Areas**:

**Environmental Management**:
- Measures are comprehensive
- Covers all relevant environmental aspects
- Mitigation strategies are appropriate
- Adaptive management approach is sound

**Monitoring Programs**:
- Monitoring frequency is adequate
- Parameters are appropriate
- Methods are scientifically sound
- Reporting is timely and comprehensive

**Compliance**:
- All certificate conditions addressed
- Previous commitments being met
- Outstanding issues resolved
- Regulatory requirements satisfied

**Feasibility**:
- Plans are realistic and achievable
- Resources are adequate
- Timeline is reasonable
- Contingencies are in place

#### IEM Reports

**Key Evaluation Areas**:

**Independence**:
- IEM is truly independent
- No conflicts of interest
- Objective assessment provided
- Willing to identify issues

**Thoroughness**:
- Site visits are adequate
- Inspections are comprehensive
- All relevant areas covered
- Documentation is complete

**Compliance Assessment**:
- Accurate evaluation of compliance
- Non-compliance issues identified
- Root causes analyzed
- Corrective actions appropriate

**Recommendations**:
- Recommendations are practical
- Address identified issues
- Follow-up is tracked
- Improvements are implemented

#### Consultation Records

**Key Evaluation Areas**:

**Consultation Activities**:
- Activities are meaningful
- Frequency is appropriate
- Methods are culturally appropriate
- Capacity support provided

**Issue Documentation**:
- Concerns clearly documented
- Context provided
- Significance assessed
- Trends identified

**Responsiveness**:
- Responses are timely
- Responses are substantive
- Commitments are specific
- Follow-through is demonstrated

**Relationship Quality**:
- Respectful engagement
- Trust is being built
- Communication is open
- Disputes are resolved constructively

## Manager Approval Process

### Manager's Role

**Responsibilities**:
- Review technical assessment
- Consider policy implications
- Assess risk and precedent
- Make final approval decision
- Ensure consistency across projects

**Manager's Perspective**:
- Broader than technical review
- Considers organizational priorities
- Balances competing interests
- Ensures defensible decisions

### Manager Review Steps

**Step 1: Review Package**

**Manager Actions**:
1. Read technical review summary
2. Review key items in package
3. Understand reviewer recommendations
4. Identify any concerns

**Duration**: 2-3 days

**Step 2: Consider Context**

**Manager Actions**:
1. Review project history
2. Consider previous submissions
3. Assess compliance track record
4. Evaluate relationship with proponent

**Duration**: 1-2 days

**Step 3: Assess Risk**

**Manager Actions**:
1. Identify potential risks
2. Evaluate likelihood and consequences
3. Consider mitigation measures
4. Determine acceptability of risk

**Risk Factors**:
- Environmental risk
- Compliance risk
- Reputational risk
- Legal risk
- Precedent implications

**Step 4: Make Decision**

**Manager Actions**:
1. Decide to approve, reject, or request more information
2. Document decision rationale
3. Provide direction to staff
4. Approve communication to proponent

**Decision Criteria**:
- Technical adequacy confirmed
- Compliance requirements met
- Risk is acceptable
- Decision is defensible
- Consistent with policy

**Step 5: Communicate Decision**

**Manager Actions**:
1. Approve final communication
2. Ensure clarity of message
3. Provide clear next steps
4. Set expectations

## Review Tools and Features

### Review Interface

**Package View**:
- Complete package overview
- Item-by-item navigation
- Document viewer
- Form data display
- Version history

**Review Panel**:
- Add review notes
- Set item status
- Request information
- Approve/reject items
- Track review progress

**Collaboration Tools**:
- Assign co-reviewers
- Share notes with team
- Discuss issues internally
- Coordinate feedback

### Review Notes

**Purpose**:
- Document evaluation
- Communicate with proponent
- Record decision rationale
- Track issues over time

**Note Components**:
- Item being reviewed
- Issue or observation
- Severity (critical, major, minor)
- Recommendation
- Visibility (staff only or public)

**Best Practices**:
- Be specific and clear
- Provide constructive feedback
- Reference certificate conditions
- Suggest solutions
- Maintain professional tone

### Internal Documents

**Purpose**:
Staff can create internal documents for:
- Technical analysis
- Risk assessments
- Consultation with experts
- Decision memoranda
- Background research

**Characteristics**:
- Not visible to proponents
- Support decision-making
- Provide detailed analysis
- Document rationale

**Examples**:
- Species impact analysis
- Water quality assessment
- Compliance history summary
- Legal opinion
- Policy interpretation

### Status Tracking

**Item Status**:
Each item can have independent status:
- **New**: Not yet reviewed
- **Under Review**: Being evaluated
- **Approved**: Meets requirements
- **Revision Required**: Needs changes
- **Rejected**: Does not meet requirements

**Package Status**:
Overall package status reflects review stage:
- **Submitted**: Awaiting review
- **Under Consultation Check**: Stage 1
- **Passed Consultation Check**: Moving to technical review
- **Under Review**: Stage 2
- **Awaiting Manager Approval**: Stage 3
- **Approved**: Final approval
- **Revision Required**: Changes needed
- **Rejected**: Not approved

## Decision Types and Outcomes

### Approval

**Meaning**: Submission meets all requirements and is accepted.

**Criteria**:
- All items approved
- No outstanding issues
- Compliance requirements met
- Adequate environmental protection
- Feasible commitments

**Process**:
1. Manager approves recommendation
2. Package status changed to "Approved"
3. Approval letter generated
4. Proponent notified
5. Package archived as approved

**Proponent Next Steps**:
- Implement approved plans
- Maintain compliance
- Submit future reports as required
- Reference approval in future submissions

**EAO Next Steps**:
- Monitor implementation
- Track compliance
- Use as baseline for future reviews
- Update project files

### Revision Required

**Meaning**: Submission has issues that must be addressed before approval.

**Criteria**:
- Some items need improvement
- Information is missing or inadequate
- Approaches need strengthening
- Issues are correctable

**Process**:
1. Reviewer identifies issues
2. Detailed feedback prepared
3. Package status changed to "Revision Required"
4. Feedback sent to proponent
5. Proponent creates new version
6. Resubmission reviewed

**Feedback Includes**:
- Specific issues identified
- What needs to change
- Why changes are needed
- Guidance on how to address
- Timeline expectations

**Common Reasons**:
- Insufficient detail
- Missing information
- Inadequate monitoring
- Unclear commitments
- Non-compliance with conditions
- Outdated data

**Proponent Next Steps**:
1. Review feedback carefully
2. Address all identified issues
3. Create new package version
4. Make required changes
5. Document what was changed
6. Resubmit for review

**EAO Next Steps**:
1. Review resubmission
2. Verify changes were made
3. Assess adequacy of revisions
4. Make final decision

### Rejection

**Meaning**: Submission does not meet requirements and is not accepted.

**Criteria**:
- Fundamental deficiencies
- Serious compliance concerns
- Inadequate environmental protection
- Unfeasible commitments
- Repeated failures to address issues

**Process**:
1. Reviewer recommends rejection
2. Manager reviews recommendation
3. Decision rationale documented
4. Package status changed to "Rejected"
5. Rejection letter sent to proponent
6. Next steps communicated

**Rejection Reasons**:
- Non-compliance with certificate
- Inadequate environmental protection
- Failure to address previous issues
- Lack of consultation
- Unfeasible or unrealistic plans
- Serious environmental risks

**Proponent Next Steps**:
- Understand rejection reasons
- Develop fundamentally different approach
- May need to create entirely new submission
- May need to engage consultants
- May need to meet with EAO

**EAO Next Steps**:
- Offer to meet with proponent
- Provide guidance on path forward
- Monitor for resubmission
- Consider enforcement if needed

## Update Requests

### What is an Update Request?

**Definition**: A request from EAO for additional information or minor changes without requiring full resubmission.

**Purpose**:
- Obtain clarification
- Get updated information
- Request minor additions
- Seek additional detail

**Different from Revision Required**:
- Less formal than revision
- Doesn't require new package version
- Quicker turnaround
- For minor issues only

### When Update Requests are Used

**Appropriate Situations**:
- Need clarification on submitted information
- Request updated contact information
- Ask for additional supporting document
- Seek more detail on specific point
- Request minor correction

**Not Appropriate For**:
- Major changes to plans
- Significant additional work
- Fundamental redesign
- Multiple substantial issues

### Update Request Process

**Step 1: EAO Creates Request**

**Staff Actions**:
1. Identify need for additional information
2. Create update request in system
3. Specify what is needed
4. Set deadline for response
5. Send to proponent

**Step 2: Proponent Receives Request**

**Proponent Sees**:
- Notification email
- Update request details
- What is being requested
- Deadline for response
- How to respond

**Step 3: Proponent Responds**

**Proponent Actions**:
1. Review request
2. Gather requested information
3. Upload documents or provide information
4. Submit response through system

**Step 4: EAO Reviews Response**

**Staff Actions**:
1. Review provided information
2. Determine if request is satisfied
3. Continue review process
4. May request additional information if needed

### Update Request Examples

**Example 1: Contact Update**
- **Request**: "Please provide updated contact information for the environmental manager position."
- **Response**: Proponent provides new contact details
- **Outcome**: Information updated, review continues

**Example 2: Additional Document**
- **Request**: "Please provide the referenced monitoring protocol document."
- **Response**: Proponent uploads protocol document
- **Outcome**: Document reviewed, issue resolved

**Example 3: Clarification**
- **Request**: "Please clarify the monitoring frequency mentioned in Section 3. Is it monthly or quarterly?"
- **Response**: Proponent clarifies it is monthly
- **Outcome**: Ambiguity resolved, review continues

## Review Timelines and SLAs

### Standard Review Timelines

**Consultation Check**: 5-10 business days
**Technical Review**: 30-60 business days
**Manager Approval**: 5-15 business days

**Total Standard Timeline**: 40-85 business days (approximately 2-4 months)

### Factors Affecting Timeline

**Faster Reviews**:
- Routine submissions (e.g., quarterly IEM reports)
- High-quality submissions
- No issues identified
- Experienced proponents
- Simple projects

**Slower Reviews**:
- Complex submissions
- Multiple issues identified
- Novel approaches
- Incomplete information
- Need for specialist consultation
- High-risk projects

### Expedited Reviews

**When Available**:
- Time-sensitive situations
- Regulatory deadlines
- Emergency circumstances
- By mutual agreement

**Process**:
- Proponent requests expedited review
- EAO assesses feasibility
- Additional resources may be allocated
- Proponent may need to provide additional support

### Proponent Responsibilities for Timely Review

**Submit Complete Packages**:
- All items completed
- All required information included
- High-quality documentation
- Clear and organized

**Respond Promptly to Questions**:
- Monitor for EAO requests
- Provide timely responses
- Give complete answers
- Don't delay review

**Maintain Communication**:
- Respond to emails
- Attend meetings if requested
- Provide updates on progress
- Be available for questions

## Business Scenarios

### Scenario 1: Smooth Approval

**Context**: Well-prepared management plan from experienced proponent.

**Timeline**:
- Day 1: Package submitted
- Day 3: Consultation check begins
- Day 8: Consultation check passes
- Day 10: Technical review assigned
- Day 35: Technical review complete, no issues
- Day 40: Manager reviews
- Day 42: Approved

**Total**: 42 days

**Success Factors**:
- Complete submission
- High quality content
- Clear documentation
- Experienced team
- Good track record

### Scenario 2: Revision Required

**Context**: IEM report with inadequate monitoring detail.

**Timeline**:
- Day 1: Package submitted
- Day 5: Consultation check passes (not applicable)
- Day 7: Technical review begins
- Day 25: Reviewer identifies insufficient monitoring detail
- Day 28: Revision requested
- Day 45: Proponent submits revised version
- Day 50: Resubmission review begins
- Day 60: Revisions adequate
- Day 65: Manager approves

**Total**: 65 days

**Key Points**:
- Issue identified during review
- Clear feedback provided
- Proponent addressed issue
- Approved after revision

### Scenario 3: Multiple Revisions

**Context**: Consultation record with inadequate documentation.

**Timeline**:
- Day 1: Package submitted
- Day 8: Consultation check fails
- Day 10: Revision requested (need more detail)
- Day 30: First revision submitted
- Day 38: Still inadequate, second revision requested
- Day 55: Second revision submitted
- Day 60: Consultation check passes
- Day 65: Technical review begins
- Day 90: Technical review complete
- Day 95: Manager approves

**Total**: 95 days

**Key Points**:
- Multiple iterations needed
- Each revision addressed some but not all issues
- Importance of thorough initial submission
- Extended timeline due to revisions
